# Crit 7 reflection

The breakthrough was treating a library booking as a state transition rather than a coloured timetable. The supplied LibCal screenshots were useful for understanding the rooms and the frustration of scanning many rows, but copying the grid alone did not answer the question a student brings to it: “Can my group use a space for this whole period?” I moved toward an exact-range search, nearby alternatives, and a room view where each half-hour block is explicit. The most useful feedback came from trying the journey: a two-hour selection looked correct on the timetable, then vanished on the room page. Later, a guest completed the email field only to be asked for it again at login. Those failures became tests of the transitions, not just tests of individual screens.

This work changed the kind of developer I want to be. I want to regard a green build as necessary but incomplete, and to let observed confusion revise the contract. The SQLite transaction protects conflicts and a two-hour daily limit; the browser tests protect the handoff between search, selection, booking and session. Neither can tell me whether the interface is understandable on a phone. I also learned to say what the system cannot honestly promise: this prototype does not reserve an ANU Library room, and email-only sign-in does not prove who owns an inbox. A trustworthy developer makes those limits visible rather than hiding them behind a polished confirmation page.
